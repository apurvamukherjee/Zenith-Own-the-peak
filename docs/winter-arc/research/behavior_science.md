# Behavioral science of streaks, commitment devices, and harsh motivation (for the 61-day "Winter Arc")

Evidence grades used throughout: **[META]** meta-analysis · **[RCT]** randomized experiment (lab or field) · **[OBS]** observational / company analytics / self-reported platform data · **[ANEC]** practitioner claim, press summary, or anecdote. "(not re-verified)" = figure recalled from the literature, linked to the DOI, but not confirmed from a fetched source this session; treat with care.

Planned design under evaluation: 61 days (Nov 1 → Dec 31, 2026; Nov 1 2026 is a Sunday), 5-tier escalating "rude" notifications (tier 5 = profanity, opt-in), **no streak freezes**, a daily mandatory drill with "debt" for missed days, a written vow, and a skip gate requiring typing "I am soft."

---

## 1. Streak psychology: do streaks drive behavior, what happens when they break, and does forgiveness (freezes/make-up days) help?

### Takeaway
Streak displays do raise engagement, but a *broken* streak lowers engagement below what the person's actual history would predict, especially when they blame themselves. A repair mechanism softens this harm. Built-in slack ("emergency reserves") and rewards for the comeback are among the best-supported tools for persistence. Removing all forgiveness is the plan's riskiest choice. The "debt" mechanic can serve as the needed repair path if it's designed for that.

### Cited Findings
- **[RCT, 7 studies]** Silverman & Barasch (2023, *JCR* 49(6):1095–1117). Showing people an *unbroken* streak in their activity log raised later engagement compared with showing a *broken* one, even when the total amount of past behavior was the same. Only the log's display differed. Mechanism: keeping the streak alive becomes a goal of its own. The effect was **stronger when people blamed themselves** for the break and **weaker when the streak could be "repaired."** No pooled effect size found. — [UDel repository](https://udspace.udel.edu/handle/19716/34160); [CU Boulder summary](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)
- **[ANEC, author interview]** Barasch: messages that point to a recent failure and nudge people to "get back on track" typically don't work, and people often drop off once a streak ends. She suggests **offering a new goal** instead. — [CU Boulder news](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)
- **[RCT, field, N>270, month-long step challenge]** Sharif & Shu (*OBHDP*, 2021). Goals framed with "emergency reserves" (a hard goal plus a few allowed skips) beat both Easy and Hard goals. Reserve groups reached their goals **up to 40% more often**. **Rebound rate after a missed day: 55% (weekly reserve) and 47% (monthly reserve) vs 37% (Hard).** Mechanism: after a miss, reserves keep *perceived progress* intact, which keeps commitment up. — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/); [paper PDF](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Sharif-Shu_EmergencyReserveFailure_OBHDP2019.pdf); [CHIBE listing](https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/nudging-persistence-after-failure-through-emergency-reserves/)
- **[RCT, megastudy, ~61k members of 24 Hour Fitness, 53–54 interventions, 4 weeks]** Milkman et al. (2021, *Nature*). The **top intervention was a bonus for returning after a missed workout**. It was worth 125 points (~$0.09) and raised visits **~27%**. Milkman: "We'll all slip up from time-to-time… it suggests how important it is to avoid having a series of missteps." 45% of the interventions raised weekly visits by 9–27%. — [Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/wharton-study-best-ways-boost-workout-habits); [Scimex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/which-programs-give-gym-goers-the-biggest-boost); DOI [10.1038/s41586-021-04128-4](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04128-4)
- **[RCT, field]** Beshears, Lee, Milkman, Mislavsky & Wisdom (2021, *Management Science* 67(7)). Paying only for workouts inside a fixed 2-hour daily window produced **fewer** gym visits than paying for any workout at any time. That held both during the intervention and after it ended. Routine-incentive recipients also dropped off more afterward, even when matched on how much they had exercised during the intervention. **Flexibility beat rigidity.** — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v67y2021i7p4139-4171.html); [HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/unexpected-exercise-advice-for-the-super-busy-ditch-the-rigid-routine)
- **[OBS/A-B, company blog]** Duolingo: allowing up to two equipped Streak Freezes raised daily active learners by **+0.38%** (relative). Duolingo's own framing: "this type of leeway actually helps people stay persistent." Learners who reach a 7-day streak are **3.6×** more likely to complete their course (correlational, with selection bias). — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)
- **[OBS/A-B]** Duolingo: separating the daily goal from the streak raised D14 retention **+3.3%**, DAU **+1%**, and the share of daily learners on a streak **+10.5%**. Letting a single lesson keep a streak alive raised the number of learners on 7+ day streaks by **>40%**. — [Duolingo "Improving the streak"](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)
- **[ANEC, podcast summary]** Duolingo reports running more than 600 streak experiments in 4 years. Making streaks easier to extend (one exercise) did **not** raise DAU and mostly attracted the least-engaged users. Leniency has diminishing returns. — [Lenny's podcast summary](https://www.recall.it/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)
- **[RCT/lab, classic]** The "what-the-hell" (disinhibition / abstinence-violation) effect. In Polivy & Herman's boundary model, once dieters believe they've crossed their limit, restraint collapses. In the pizza-preload paradigm, dieters who *perceived* the slice as large ate about **2× the cookies**, though actual intake was the same. Perceived failure, not actual failure, triggers it. Popular sources note it is more likely with short-horizon ("today") goals. — [Wikipedia: counterregulatory eating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterregulatory_eating); [EFPSA summary](https://more.efpsa.org/rpblog/?p=1172); [boundary model](https://tutor2u.net/psychology/topics/boundary-model)
- **[OBS, small N]** Lally et al. (2010): "missing one opportunity to perform the behaviour did not materially affect the habit formation process." Caveat: there were few missed-day data points. — [Crossref record DOI 10.1002/ejsp.674](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1002/ejsp.674); [ISPA repository](https://repositorio.ispa.pt/entities/publication/fae72a1c-b2b6-4991-9951-5d20b607f688/full)

### Inferences
- **"No streak freezes" will likely backfire on the user's first miss.** The evidence chain: a broken streak lowers engagement below baseline (Silverman & Barasch). Self-blame makes it worse, and harsh self-focused messaging promotes self-blame. A perceived failure triggers what-the-hell disinhibition. One miss doesn't hurt the habit itself (Lally). The damage comes from the psychological reaction to the break, not the missed rep.
- **Recommended tweak: frame slack as part of a hard goal, not as a "freeze."** Example: "61 days. You get 3 Emergency Reserves. Unused reserves = bonus badge." Sharif & Shu found this beats both Easy and Hard goals. It also keeps the hardcore identity, because reserves are scarce, visible, and bragged about when left unused. The current Zenith one-per-week `spendStreakFreeze()` is close in mechanics. The difference is framing: a reserve is part of the challenge's rules, not an escape hatch.
- **"Debt" is the plan's best idea if it works as streak *repair*.** Silverman & Barasch found repairability blunts the broken-streak penalty. If paying off debt visually restores the day (the chain re-links, red turns to "repaired" amber), debt becomes the repair mechanism the evidence favors. If the miss stays a permanent black mark plus extra work, it's pure punishment and adds to the what-the-hell risk.
- **Reward the comeback, not just continuity.** Milkman's top megastudy arm paid a tiny bonus for returning after a miss. Copy it: a bonus XP drop or a special badge ("Got back up") on the first completed day after a miss.
- **Don't make daily completion trivially easy.** Duolingo's one-exercise test drew low-quality engagement. Keep the drill real, but keep a "minimum viable drill" floor (see section 7).

### Gaps
- No published Duolingo result isolates streak freezes' effect on long-term retention or churn. The +0.38% DAU figure is for raising the cap from one freeze to two.
- No pooled effect size retrieved for Silverman & Barasch.
- The "elastic habits" concept (multiple completion levels per day) comes from the self-help author Stephen Guise, not from Milkman. No peer-reviewed test of it found. The closest evidence is the flexibility vs routinization result above.
- Sheeran/Milkman-style "make-up day" designs: no RCT found that tests make-up days specifically, beyond the return bonus.

## 2. Loss aversion and commitment devices: what works, and what works without money?

### Takeaway
Commitment devices work for the minority who choose them, and their effects can outlast the contract. But uptake is low, and many committers still fail. For people who do take them, the biggest gains on platforms like stickK come from **referees** (social accountability) and real stakes. With no money involved, the best available levers are a named referee/friend and a clear, measurable commitment. Public announcement of *identity* goals ("I will become a beast") can backfire.

### Cited Findings
- **[RCT, field, N≈2,000, Philippines]** Giné, Karlan & Zinman (2010, *AEJ: Applied*), CARES smoking-cessation commitment savings. **11% take-up**. People offered CARES were **~3 pp** more likely to pass a 6-month urine test, and **3.4–5.7 pp** more likely on *surprise* tests at 12 months (control pass rate 8.9–14.7%). However, **66% of those who committed still failed** and forfeited their money. — [J-PAL evaluation summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/cares-commitment-savings-smoking-cessation-philippines?lang=pt-br); [IPA paper PDF](https://es.poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/publications/put_your_money_where_your_butt_is.pdf)
- **[RCT, field, Fortune 500 employees]** Royer, Stehr & Sydnor (2015, *AEJ: Applied*). A 4-week gym incentive alone produced only small lasting effects. Adding an **optional self-funded commitment contract** produced gym-use changes still detectable **years** later. Only **~12%** took up the contract (self-selected). — [NBER w18580](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18580); [AEA research highlight](https://benny.aeaweb.org/research/how-can-financial-incentives-healthy-habits); [BLS MLR summary](https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2013/beyond-bls/the-skinny-on-financial-incentives-for-exercise-programs.htm)
- **[OBS, company data, ~125k contracts, heavy self-selection]** stickK's own analysis: **29%** success with no referee and no stakes, **59%** with a referee, **71.5%** with money at stake, **~80%** with both. An anti-charity clause reached **~82%**. For weight-loss contracts only, an HBS case reports **61.3%** (fully engaged: stakes plus referee) vs **14.5%** (neither). — [HarvardX course page quoting NYT](https://courses.edx.org/c4x/HarvardX/BUS5.1x/asset/8-page.html); [Washingtonian](https://washingtonian.com/?p=142968); [Yale Daily News](https://yaledailynews.com/?p=24449)
- **[OBS, academic]** An ICWSM 2024 study of stickK (742,923 users, 397,456 commitments): accountability and stakes are **rarely used but strongly predict success**. — [AAAI ICWSM](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/31440)
- **[RCT, lab, 4 experiments]** Gollwitzer, Sheeran, Michalski & Seifert (2009, *Psych Sci* 20:612–618). **Identity-related** intentions that other people *noticed* were acted on **less** intensively than unnoticed ones. Mechanism: being noticed gives a premature sense of already having the identity (self-completion theory). The effect appeared only among people strongly committed to the identity. — [Konstanz listing](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/node/131); [Stafforini abstract](https://stafforini.com/works/gollwitzer-2009-when-intentions-go/)
- **[RCT, lab]** Ariely & Wertenbroch (2002, *Psych Sci*). People voluntarily set costly self-imposed deadlines. These beat having no deadline, but did worse than evenly spaced deadlines set by someone else. People are partly, not fully, aware of their own self-control problems. (not re-verified) — DOI [10.1111/1467-9280.00441](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.00441)

### Inferences
- **The written vow is cheap and plausibly helpful, but only if it's specific and behavioral.** "I will do the drill every day before 9pm, at the gym or in my room" makes it a contract-like plan (see section 3). An identity-flavored vow ("I am a warrior") shown to friends risks the Gollwitzer 2009 premature-completion effect. Keep the vow *private by default*, and if it's shared, share the **behavior** and **progress**, not the identity claim.
- **The best no-money stake is a referee.** Zenith already has Convex follows and a leaderboard. An opt-in "Arc partner" who sees only daily done/missed (in line with the existing privacy rules: no raw logs) gives the referee effect. stickK's 29%→59% jump is the largest single lever in their data, though it's self-selected and from company data.
- **Symbolic loss aversion that fits Zenith:** a staked XP pledge (e.g., stake 500 XP at the start, earn it back with a bonus at day 61, lose a portion for unpaid debt) or a mythic "Winter Arc" badge forfeited on abandonment. No direct evidence was found that symbolic or in-app stakes work like money. Treat this as a hypothesis worth testing.
- **Expect low uptake for the hardest mode.** Commitment take-up runs about 11–12% in field studies. A profanity tier plus a "no reserves" mode will appeal to a small, self-selected group. That's fine for a single-user app, but those users are still likely to fail (66% in CARES). Design the failure path, not just the success path.

### Gaps
- No RCT found on purely symbolic stakes (badges, XP) as commitment devices. Beeminder publishes no peer-reviewed outcome data that I found.
- stickK's figures are company-reported and confounded by selection. They can't tell whether referees cause success or motivated people pick referees.

## 3. Implementation intentions, habit timelines (Lally 66 days vs a 61-day arc), habit stacking, temptation bundling

### Takeaway
If-then planning is one of the best-supported, cheapest interventions (medium-to-large effect in the 2006 meta-analysis). The vow screen should capture when/where/if-then plans. Sixty-one days is close to the 66-day *median* time to automaticity, but the range is huge (18–254). So the arc should be framed as building a habit, not proof of having one, and should hand off into a maintenance mode, because most intervention effects fade once the program ends.

### Cited Findings
- **[META, 94 tests]** Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006, *Adv. Exp. Soc. Psych.* 38:69–119). Implementation intentions ("If situation X, then I will do Y") had **d = 0.65** on goal attainment (medium-to-large). — [Konstanz KOPS](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f)
- **[META, 642 tests]** Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer (2024) is a much larger follow-up. My recollection is that its pooled effect is smaller (~d = 0.27) (not re-verified). — [goalsandprogress summary](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-research/)
- **[OBS, N=96, 39 with good fit, 12 weeks]** Lally et al. (2010, *EJSP*): median **66 days** to reach 95% of the automaticity plateau, **range 18–254**. The upper range is extrapolated beyond the 84-day study window. More consistent performance gave better model fit. One missed opportunity didn't materially affect the process. Not yet replicated. — [Surrey interview with Lally](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/does-it-really-take-66-days-form-habit-we-asked-expert-dr-pippa-lally); [Behavioral Scientist explainer](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/how-long-to-form-a-habit); [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1002/ejsp.674)
- **[RCT, N=226]** Milkman, Minson & Volpp (2014, *Mgmt Sci*), temptation bundling. Gym-only access to tempting audiobooks raised gym visits **51%** (full bundling) and **29%** (encouraged bundling) vs control. **Effects decayed**, notably after Thanksgiving. **61%** later paid for gym-only iPod access. — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/TemptationBundling.pdf); [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/oid_papers/150)
- **[RCT, follow-up]** In a larger follow-up, *encouraging* temptation bundling had only a **modest** added effect over just giving audiobooks. — [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/researchers-used-hunger-games-encourage-healthier-choices/); [SUFE listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/393752)
- **[RCT, megastudy]** Most effects of the 4-week megastudy interventions did not persist after the intervention ended. One report says only **~8%** of interventions produced measurable change afterward. — [Scimex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/which-programs-give-gym-goers-the-biggest-boost)
- **[RCT]** Flexible incentives built more durable exercise than rigid same-time routines (Beshears et al. 2021, above). — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v67y2021i7p4139-4171.html)

### Inferences
- **Turn the vow into an if-then plan.** Add 2–3 required fields to the vow: *When* (time cue), *Where*, and *If I'm tempted to skip, then I will…* (e.g., "do the 5-minute minimum version"). This is the highest-value, lowest-cost tweak in this document. The fields can also schedule the drill as a Tasks/Events item with `remindBefore`, which already exists in Zenith.
- **Habit stacking** (Fogg's "after I [existing habit], I will…") is practitioner advice [ANEC] but is mechanically an implementation intention with an existing-routine cue. Offer it as a vow template ("After I brush my teeth at night, I do the drill").
- **Don't promise "a habit in 61 days."** The honest message is "61 days ≈ the median time it takes; some need longer." Plan a **post-arc maintenance mode** (Jan: lighter cadence, still tracked) because intervention effects fade at program end (megastudy, temptation bundling decay).
- **Don't force a fixed drill time.** Beshears et al. suggest you shouldn't penalize doing the drill at 11pm instead of 7am. Time cues help planning, but completion should count any time that local day (using `todayKey()`).
- **Temptation bundling option:** "Only listen to [podcast/playlist] during the drill." Cheap to suggest in the vow. Expect modest returns.

### Gaps
- The exact pooled d from the 2024 642-test meta-analysis wasn't confirmed this session.
- No direct RCT evidence on habit stacking as distinct from implementation intentions.

## 4. Harsh, shaming, and guilt messaging: shame vs guilt, self-compassion vs self-criticism, reactance, fear appeals, notification fatigue, opt-in harshness, humor

### Takeaway
Messages that target the **behavior** ("you skipped; fix it now," i.e. guilt) can motivate repair. Messages that target the **self** ("you're pathetic/soft," i.e. shame) predict hiding, avoidance, and giving up. Self-criticism predicts *less* goal progress. Self-compassion after failure *increases* effort to improve. Controlling, forceful language triggers reactance. Fear and threat messages work modestly *only* when paired with a clear, doable action. The user opting in to harshness probably reduces reactance (autonomy), but no study tests opted-in profanity directly.

### Cited Findings
- **[Review]** Tangney, Stuewig & Mashek (2007, *Annu. Rev. Psychol.*). Shame (a negative evaluation of the self) is linked to hiding/escape, defensiveness, and anger. Guilt (a negative evaluation of a specific behavior) is linked to reparative action. (not re-verified) — DOI [10.1146/annurev.psych.56.091103.070145](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.56.091103.070145)
- **[Physical-activity specific]** Guilt follows transgressions attributed to *lack of effort* and motivates more effort. Shame follows transgressions attributed to *inability* and leads to **goal disengagement**. — [JEMS / SCAPPS abstract](https://www.scapps.org/jems/index.php/1/article/view/1236)
- **[RCT, 4 experiments]** Breines & Chen (2012, *PSPB* 38(9):1133–43). Self-compassion after failure, compared with self-esteem-boosting or neutral conditions, produced more belief that weaknesses can change, more motivation to make amends, and **more time studying after a failed test**. Press reports put it at ~25% longer (secondary source). — [PubMed 22645164](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22645164/); [Globe and Mail](https://beta.theglobeandmail.com/report-on-business/careers/management/whats-better-self-esteem-or-self-compassion/article4874621/)
- **[OBS, multi-study; META-like thesis review]** Powers, Koestner & Zuroff (2007, *JSCP* 26:814–828) and Powers et al. (2011, *PSPB*, 5 studies): self-criticism is linked to **less goal progress**. A review of 17 samples (N=3,044) found self-critical people make less progress on personal goals and are more driven by internal pressure. — [SAGE PSPB 2011](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0146167211410246); [Carleton Goal Lab PDF](https://www.carleton.ca/goallab/wp-content/uploads/The-effects-of-self-criticism-and-self-oriented-perfectionism-on-goal-pursuit..pdf); [Glasgow thesis review](https://theses.gla.ac.uk/5684/1/2014larkindclinpsy.pdf)
- **[META, 127 articles, 248 samples, N=27,372]** Tannenbaum et al. (2015, *Psych Bull* 141(6)): fear appeals had **d = 0.29** on attitudes/intentions/behavior. They were **stronger with efficacy statements** (a clear, doable action) and for **one-time** rather than repeated behaviors. No backfire conditions were identified. A re-analysis by Kok et al. disputes some of the pooling. — [PubMed 26501228](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26501228/); [Kok re-analysis dataset](https://b2find.eudat.eu/dataset/a0b556f9-bd68-5030-a646-67e5dbe0b254)
- **[META, 33 studies, 146 effects, 2005–2024]** Ma et al. (2025/26, *Human Communication Research*). High freedom-threatening (controlling/forceful) language increased **anger r = .21**, **negative cognitions r = .17**, and **reactance r = .20** vs low-threat language. Anger and negative cognitions were in turn negatively associated with persuasion. — [HKBU scholars listing](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/message-effects-on-psychological-reactance-meta-analyses/); [HKBU news](https://research.hkbu.edu.hk/news/ditch-the-directives-why-bossy-messaging-backfires-in-the-fight-for-public-persuasion)
- **[META, 20 studies, N=4,942]** Rains (2013, *HCR* 39(1)) supports the "intertwined" model: reactance is a mix of anger and counterarguing. — [OUP abstract](https://academic.oup.com/hcr/article-abstract/39/1/47/4093682)
- **[RCT, lab]** Wood, Perunovic & Lee (2009, *Psych Sci* 20:860–866). Repeating self-statements that a person doesn't believe made **low-self-esteem** participants feel *worse*. People respond to self-statements differently depending on their existing self-view. — [PubMed 19493324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19493324/)
- **[OBS, vendor survey]** Braze: ~25% of users who opted out of push said it was because they got too many. — [Braze](https://www.braze.com/resources/articles/opt-out-of-push-notifications-why-users-do-it)
- **[OBS, vendor, 63M users]** Urban Airship: users getting daily or more frequent pushes had the *highest* retention, and under-messaging was the bigger risk. A MoEngage report says **irrelevant** pushes drive uninstalls. Relevance matters more than volume. — [Urban Airship benchmark PDF](https://grow.urbanairship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/WP_App_Retention_Rates_Benchmarks.pdf); [MoEngage report](https://info.moengage.com/hubfs/Retention%20Benchmark%20reports/e-commerce-app-india-report_final.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Make the 5 tiers escalate in intensity of guilt, not shame.** Every tier, profanity included, should attack the *skipped drill* and end with a concrete action ("Drill's not done. 12 minutes. Go."). Never attack the *person* ("you're weak/worthless"). This matches Tangney's distinction and the fear-appeal efficacy finding. Profanity aimed at the situation ("it's 10pm and the damn drill is still sitting there") is far safer than profanity aimed at the user.
- **Don't escalate into someone who has already lapsed.** After a miss, the risky window for what-the-hell and shame-driven disengagement is the next day. That's exactly when a naive escalation would send its harshest message. Suggest: escalation runs *within* a day (reminder → nag → roast as the deadline nears), then **resets** the next morning to a **comeback-framed** message ("Debt: 1 drill. Clear it today and the chain re-links."). This draws on Breines & Chen, Barasch's "offer a new goal," and Milkman's return bonus.
- **Opt-in and user-chosen tone probably cut reactance.** Reactance comes from threatened *freedom*, and choosing the tone yourself is an exercise of freedom. This is inference only, with no direct study. Keep it reversible in one tap ("Too much? Dial it down"). A tone the user can't escape becomes controlling again.
- **Humor softens the reactance risk** [ANEC]. Duolingo's unhinged-owl voice is widely cited as effective, but I found no published outcome data. "Roast" works better than "abuse." Pre-write the copy and review it against the guilt-vs-shame test.
- **Frequency:** relevance beats volume. Tie each push to state (drill not done yet, deadline approaching) rather than a fixed count, and cap at roughly 3–5 per day. Never push once the drill is done.

### Gaps
- No study found that tests **user-opted-in harsh or profane coaching** against neutral messaging. "Tough love" coaching evidence is mostly practitioner opinion.
- No published Duolingo data on whether guilt-trip notifications raise retention, net of opt-outs.
- No independent (non-vendor) figure on notification-driven uninstall rates.

## 5. Fresh-start effect: Nov 1 start, New Year end, mid-arc resets

### Takeaway
Temporal landmarks (new week, month, year, semester, birthday, holiday) reliably spike goal-pursuit behavior, including gym visits. Nov 1 (a new month) is a decent launch day. Dec 1 is a natural mid-arc fresh start, a good place to reset the narrative for anyone who has slipped. Jan 1 right after the arc is the strongest fresh start of the year, so use it to hand off into the next phase rather than letting the arc end in a void.

### Cited Findings
- **[OBS, 3 studies]** Dai, Milkman & Riis (2014, *Mgmt Sci*): Google searches for "diet," **university gym visits**, and goal commitments (stickK) all rise after temporal landmarks: the start of a week, month, year, or semester, a birthday, or a holiday. Proposed mechanism: landmarks open new "mental accounting periods" that relegate past failures to a previous self. — [Wharton PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf); [APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/minds-business/why-monday-is-the-best-day-for-setting-new-goals.html)
- **[ANEC/author]** Barasch recommends offering a **new goal** after a streak breaks, rather than dwelling on the failure. — [CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)

### Inferences
- Nov 1 2026 is a Sunday. That's both a new month and the start of a week for people who count Sunday as day one; for Monday-starters, Nov 2 would add the "new week" landmark. Either works. Keep the 61-day count intact (Nov 1 → Dec 31 = 30 + 31 = 61).
- **Dec 1 "Second Wind" checkpoint:** a deliberate fresh-start moment, framed as "Phase 2 of 2." For users with debt or a broken chain, offer a one-time reframe ("New month. Debt capped / old misses archived as Phase 1"). This applies the Dai et al. mechanism to the what-the-hell risk.
- Weekly landmarks can serve as sub-goals: 61 days ≈ 9 "weeks of winter," each with its own mini progress bar (see section 6).
- **Jan 1 handoff:** the arc ends Dec 31, right before the strongest fresh-start landmark of the year. Ending screen: "Arc complete → set your 2027 cadence." That captures the January spike for maintenance.

### Gaps
- Exact percentage lifts in gym visits per landmark weren't extracted (they're in the full paper's tables).
- No RCT found on *designed* (artificial) fresh-start resets in apps. One known related paper is Beshears, Dai, Milkman & Benartzi (2021) on fresh-start framing for savings (not re-verified).

## 6. Goal gradient, progress bars, endowed progress, variable rewards

### Takeaway
People speed up as a goal gets close, and an artificial head start increases completion. A 61-day bar risks a "stuck in the middle" slump (days ~20–45). The fix is nested sub-goals (weeks, a Dec 1 phase) plus endowed progress (count the vow or prep as progress).

### Cited Findings
- **[RCT, field]** Nunes & Drèze (2006, *JCR*), car-wash loyalty cards. A 10-stamp card with 2 stamps pre-filled was completed far more often than an 8-stamp card with none, even though both required 8 washes. Roughly **twice as many** completions. The commonly cited figures, **34% vs 19%**, are not re-verified. The effect is stronger when the head start comes with a reason. — [ThinkInsights summary](https://thinkinsights.net/consulting/endowed-progress-effect); [Wharton podcast](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/podcast/knowledge-at-wharton-podcast/the-lowdown-on-customer-loyalty-programs-which-are-the-most-effective-and-why/); DOI [10.1086/500480](https://doi.org/10.1086/500480)
- **[RCT/OBS, field]** Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng (2006, *JMR*), goal-gradient. Coffee-card customers bought more often as they neared the reward (not re-verified). — DOI [10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39)
- **[RCT, field]** Sharif & Shu: reserves preserve *perceived progress* after a miss, and perceived progress drove commitment. — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/emergency-reserves/)
- **[OBS/A-B]** Duolingo: streak-milestone animations raised 7-day retention of new learners **+1.7%**. Duolingo notes that a streak's relative growth shrinks over time (day 2→3 is +50%, day 200→201 is +0.5%), which is why they add milestones. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)

### Inferences
- **Endowed progress:** count "Vow signed" (and an optional Oct 31 "prep day") as visible progress. The bar shouldn't start at 0/61. Give the head start a reason ("Vow = Day 0 done").
- **Nested gradients:** show a weekly ring (x/7) alongside the 61-day bar, with a Dec 1 "halfway" milestone and celebrations at 7/14/30/45/61. That keeps a nearby goal always in view.
- **Debt should show as "progress at risk," not "progress lost."** Show repaired days as filled (a different color), to protect perceived progress.
- **Variable rewards:** use XP or loot drops sparingly, e.g. a random "bonus drill XP." No peer-reviewed evidence was found specifically for variable-ratio rewards in fitness apps, and Zenith already has a 69-badge system. A few hidden Winter Arc mystery badges (in line with the existing pattern) are a cheap fit.

### Gaps
- Exact Nunes & Drèze completion percentages, and the "stuck in the middle" literature (Bonezzi, Brendl & De Angelis 2011), weren't verified this session.
- No peer-reviewed evidence found on variable or random rewards in habit apps specifically.

## 7. Mental-health safety: punitive self-tracking, exercise compulsion, overtraining, guardrails

### Takeaway
Fitness tracking correlates with compulsive exercise and disordered eating, most strongly among people who track to control weight or shape. A "no excuses, debt accumulates, type 'I am soft' to skip" system can reinforce exactly those patterns in vulnerable users and push people to train while sick or injured. Medical exemptions that don't count as misses, capped debt, and a non-shaming skip path are the minimum guardrails.

### Cited Findings
- **[OBS, cross-sectional]** Loughborough (Plateau et al.): ~65% of young people surveyed used fitness or food trackers. Users had higher disordered eating and compulsive-exercise scores, highest among those tracking to manage weight or shape. The authors say tracking probably doesn't *cause* eating disorders but may reinforce them in vulnerable people. — [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/fitness-trackers-and-eating-disorders-is-there-a-link-121663); [Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/field/935320/scroll_in)
- **[OBS]** Simpson & Mazzeo (*Eating Behaviors*): fitness tracking (but not calorie tracking) was a unique indicator of eating-disorder symptoms. — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/za/blog/the-truth-about-exercise-addiction/201702/do-fitness-trackers-promote-eating-disorders)
- **[RCT]** Etkin (2016, *JCR*, "The Hidden Cost of Personal Quantification"): measuring an activity can make it feel more like work, which lowers enjoyment and continuation (not re-verified). — DOI [10.1093/jcr/ucw042](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucw042)
- **[RCT]** Wood et al. (2009): self-statements contrary to a person's self-view make low-self-esteem people feel worse. This is relevant to forcing "I am soft." — [PubMed 19493324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19493324/)
- **[Review/OBS]** Self-criticism predicts less goal progress, and shame predicts disengagement (section 4). — [Glasgow thesis review](https://theses.gla.ac.uk/5684/1/2014larkindclinpsy.pdf); [JEMS](https://www.scapps.org/jems/index.php/1/article/view/1236)

### Inferences
- **The "I am soft" gate is the second-riskiest element** (after "no freezes"). Friction before skipping is good: it makes skipping deliberate rather than automatic. But forcing a self-labeling statement is a shame manipulation (self-focused), and Wood et al. suggest it lands worst on users already low on self-worth. Lower-risk alternatives that keep the friction:
  - Type a **behavioral, guilt-framed** statement: "I'm skipping today. I owe 1 drill." That's accurate, focused on repair, and still uncomfortable.
  - Or make the gate **offer the minimum drill first** ("5-minute version counts → do it now?"), then require an **if-then plan for tomorrow** to confirm the skip. That turns the lapse into an implementation intention.
  - If "I am soft" stays, make it a tier-5-only, opt-in flavor alongside the profanity. The default should be the behavioral version.
- **Separate "excuses" from "exemptions."** Keep "no freezes for laziness" if that's the identity, but add a **Sick / Injury / Medical pause** that pauses the arc clock (or marks days "exempt," neither counted as missed nor as debt). Optionally require a one-line reason, and it can't be used retroactively more than one day. Without this, the design pushes users to train while sick or injured, which is a real physical harm.
- **Cap debt.** Unlimited debt snowballs into "it's hopeless" (what-the-hell) and into overtraining via catch-up binges. Suggestions: at most 1 extra drill repaid per day, at most N (e.g., 3) outstanding. Beyond the cap, old debt converts to a visible "scar" on the calendar rather than more owed reps. Optionally reset the cap on Dec 1 (fresh start).
- **Mandatory rest compatibility:** the daily drill must be light or mobility-scale (or have a "recovery drill" variant) so it doesn't conflict with the user's lifting rest days or planned deload weeks. Zenith already detects deload weeks (`detectDeloadWeek()`) and has rest-day types in its data model (`restDayLogs`).
- **Escape hatches stay one tap away:** downgrade tone, exit the arc gracefully (end with a "Phase complete: X/61" summary, not a "quitter" screen), and make "quiet hours" respect sleep (no tier-5 pushes after the user's typical bedtime, from `sleepTargetMin` / sleep logs).
- **Watch for warning signals** (inference, no validated thresholds found): drill completion logged on sick-flagged days, repeated debt repayment at the cap, very low sleep alongside high training. Respond with a gentle check-in instead of escalation.

### Gaps
- No RCT found on punitive or streak mechanics *causing* exercise compulsion. The evidence is cross-sectional correlation.
- No validated in-app screening thresholds for compulsive exercise were found. Exercise-addiction prevalence figures weren't retrieved this session.
- No research found on "sick-day mode" designs in consumer apps. The recommendation rests on inference from the overtraining and exercise-compulsion risk plus the streak-repair findings.
